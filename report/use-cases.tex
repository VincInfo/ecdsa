\subsubsection{Electronic Governance (E-governance)}

Electronic governance (e-governance) leverages technologies like the Internet, SMS, and email to streamline government services for citizens, businesses, and government bodies. It encompasses a variety of transactions including government to citizens (G2C), business (G2B), employees (G2E), and intergovernmental (G2G) interactions, aiming to enhance transparency, flexibility, and manageability of services.

However, the adoption of e-governance also presents significant security challenges, with platforms exposed to various threats such as hacking and natural disasters. To mitigate these risks, comprehensive security measures including server, network, and data security are implemented. Advanced cryptographic methods like Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC) and the Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm (ECDSA) are particularly crucial for protecting sensitive data and ensuring secure communications within and between government entities. These technologies are key in maintaining the integrity and privacy of e-governance transactions, securing them against a wide array of cyber threats.

\subsection{Transportation and Infrastructure}
\label{use-cases:transportation-and-infrastructure}

\subsubsection{Electronic-Vehicular Networks (VANETs)}

Vehicular ad hoc networks (VANET) significantly improve road safety and efficiency by enabling dynamic communication between vehicles and road infrastructure. These networks leverage self-regulation and distributed communication environments to effectively manage traffic, prevent accidents, and enforce compliance with road laws. VANET systems operate primarily in two modes: vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V) and vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2I), functioning as a specialized form of mobile ad hoc networks (MANET) with structured interactions.

Security poses a major challenge in VANETs due to the openness of the communication medium, which exposes the network to various cyber threats. To safeguard data, VANETs employ encryption and digital signature protocols, notably the Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm (ECDSA). This cryptographic method helps authenticate messages, ensuring their integrity and non-repudiation while being efficient enough for constrained resource environments. The implementation of ECDSA and its effectiveness in VANETs depends crucially on the selection of cryptographic curves and the balance between key size and the efficiency of signature processes.

\subsubsection{Secure Shell (SSH)}

Secure Shell (SSH) employs Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC) in three crucial ways to enhance network security and integrity, for example in the SSH-2 protocol. Firstly, SSH-2 utilizes the Diffie-Hellman key exchange algorithm to generate equivalent keys \footnote{\label{source-exceeded}The content provided by ChatGPT exceeded the source material and was therefore condensed.}. During this process, each server is equipped with a unique host key, which is fundamental for the server-client authentication phase. This key is sent to the client during the key exchange, allowing the client to verify its authenticity by comparing the key's fingerprint against a stored value.

Moreover, the server substantiates its identity by signing a transcript of the key exchanges. This signature, potentially created using the Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm (ECDSA), certifies the integrity of the recorded exchanges and ensures that the communication remains secure. Additionally, this use of ECDSA is not limited to server authentication. Clients also employ ECDSA public keys to authenticate each other. \footref{source-exceeded}

\subsubsection{Transport Layer Security (TLS)}

Elliptic curves play a significant role in the application of the Transport Layer Security (TLS) protocol, as detailed in RFC 4492. This protocol specification includes the use of the Diffie-Hellman key exchange algorithm, which is based on elliptic curves. Known as ECDH (Elliptic Curve Diffie-Hellman), this method of key exchange utilizes keys of two distinct types. The first type is long-term keys, which allow for multiple key exchanges to reuse the same keys. The second type is short-lived keys, where each key exchange leads to the generation of new keys.

\subsection{Digital Documentation and Cryptography}

% ----------------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection{Signing PDF Documents}

The Portable Document Format (PDF) is widely used for document management and increasingly for digital signatures, as more aspects of life transition to digital formats. Despite their growing significance, it wasn't until PDF 2.0 that the PDF standards began to support the Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm (ECDSA), a method ensuring more secure digital signatures.

Adobe Acrobat, a leading PDF application, has been a pioneer in this area, supporting ECDSA signatures since version 11.0. Adobe’s early adoption of ECDSA has served as a de facto reference for other PDF tools and applications, facilitating the use of modern cryptographic techniques for signing PDF documents securely. \cite{Gavenda2021}
